# A claim's scope travels with it: a date for one platform is not a date for the others, and a true fact can still mislead

**One-liner:** Verified facts arrive with qualifiers: *for PS5*, *in the US and select regions*, *never released outside China*. When copy is written from them, the qualifier is the first thing dropped, because it makes the sentence longer. The result is a sentence built only from confirmed facts that states something no source says. Carry every qualifier into the copy, or drop the claim.

## The shape

Two failures in one post, both from facts that were individually confirmed:

1. **Scope widened.** The publisher's footnote: "launches on October 29*, for PlayStation 5. *October 28 in the US and select regions." Steam, for PC, lists only "Oct 28" with no regional note. The copy said "It unlocks on PS5 **and PC** on October 28 in the US and select regions, and October 29 everywhere else." Every number was sourced, but the PS5 split had been carried over to PC. Two checker passes approved it. A third, told to confirm every sentence word for word against the raw page text, caught it.
2. **True, but misleading by implication.** The founder wrote that his first indie game "grew into a whole franchise and picked up a new name: Phantom Blade. Most of these games were for smartphones and never released outside of China." The caption said the series "grew into Phantom Blade" and asked "Did you ever play one of the Phantom Blade games before this one?" The account owner, who had never heard of those games, read it as made up. It wasn't, but for a Western audience the question implied games they could have played and almost certainly never could. The qualifier "never released outside of China" was what made the fact honest, and the copy dropped it.

## What to do

- **Verifiers return the qualifier as part of the claim:** platform, region, edition, audience, "according to whom", "as of when". A claim row without its scope is incomplete.
- **Before writing, list each claim with its scope, and check the sentence doesn't widen it.** "On PS5 in the US and select regions (October 29 elsewhere); out October 28 on Steam" is longer than "October 28 on PS5 and PC", and it is what the sources say.
- **Read the copy as the audience, not the researcher.** For each true sentence, ask what a reader who knows nothing else will infer. If the honest version needs a qualifier the copy can't fit, cut the sentence.
- **Credit first-person accounts as accounts:** "he wrote in 2023 that it started in 2010", not "it started in 2010", when the only source is the person themselves.
- **The final check is sentence-by-sentence against raw text.** A summarising fetch tool and a checker reading the source log both approved the widened date; only re-opening the raw page for each sentence found it.

## Case (Playmoir @playmoir, Phantom Blade Zero launch post, 2026-10-27)

The PlayStation Blog State of Play post (Aug 17, 2026) carries the PS5 footnote. Steam app 4115450 lists `Oct 28, 2026`. The founder's post (PlayStation Blog, May 24, 2023) carries the China-only line. The final copy reads "Rainblood, spiritually reborn." ("reborn" alone read like a remake; his phrase is "spiritual rebirth"), credits the 2010 origin as "wrote in 2023", and leaves out the earlier games.
